# 1. Basic Sentence Structure

Huntspeak's basic word order is **Subject – Verb – Object** (SVO), the same as English:

> **Î vîsat tye.**
> I see-PRESENT animal
> "I see the animal."

Rules established by the official material and the core documentation:

- **Adverbs follow the verb:** Subject, Verb (adverb), Object.

  > **Se nenad shai-du tiau tye.**
  > she moves fast-er than animal
  > "She moves faster than an animal."

- **Adjectives come before the noun they describe:** Subject, Verb, (adjective) Object.

  > **Î vîset däl tye yat wäwi.**
  > I saw many animal in forest
  > "I saw many animals in the forest."

- **With a prefix verb** (the irregular verbs of chapter 4, like "be" and "have"), the adjective comes before the verb–object unit it refers to:

  > **Î wakh varadyävu.**
  > I young have-child
  > "I have a young child."

- The subject can be left out when it is obvious - most often third person:

  > **Tye nenad.** - "The animal moves." (the pronoun *se* "it" is implied inside the verb; see chapter 3)

Questions rearrange this order (chapter 7), and linked clauses follow the patterns of chapter 10. Everything else, who acts, and when, is carried by the verb itself, which is the subject of chapters 3 and 4.
