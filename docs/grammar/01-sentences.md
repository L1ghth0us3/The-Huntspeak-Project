# 1. Basic Sentence Structure

You’ll build almost every sentence the same way English does: **Subject – Verb – Object**.

> **Î vîsat tye.**
> I see-PRESENT animal
> “I see the animal.”

A few placement habits to learn:

- **Adverbs follow the verb:** Subject, Verb (adverb), Object.

  > **Se nenad shai-du tiau tye.**
  > she moves fast-er than animal
  > “She moves faster than an animal.”

- **Adjectives come before the noun:**

  > **Î vîset däl tye yat wäwi.**
  > I saw many animal in forest
  > “I saw many animals in the forest.”

- **With prefix verbs** (“be”, “have”, and friends — chapter 4), the adjective sits before the verb–object unit:

  > **Î wakh varadyävu.**
  > I young have-child
  > “I have a young child.”

- You can **drop the subject** when it’s obvious — often third person:

  > **Tye nenad.** — “The animal moves.” (the *e* inside the verb already says “he/she/it”; see chapter 3)

Questions rearrange things (chapter 7). Linking clauses is chapter 10. Who acts and when is mostly carried inside the verb — that’s chapters 3 and 4.
