# 6. Adjectives and Comparison

Adjectives sit **before** the noun (`wakh radyävu` “a young child”). To say something *is* dark, tall, or good, use the copula prefix `sh-` (`sha-sil` “is dark” - chapter 4).

## Comparative and superlative

Two suffixes:

- **-du** - comparative (“more X”, “X-er”)
- **-vu** - superlative (“most X”, “X-est”)

Compare with **tiau** “than”:

> **Kharau hâiru sha-sil-du tiau nätum.**
> this cave is-dark-er than night
> “This cave is darker than the night.”

> **Su amî qar-vu sha-shidyävu.**
> you my good-est be-friend
> “You are my best friend.”

Comparatives can also follow the verb as adverbs:

> **Se nenad shai-du tiau tye.**
> she moves fast-er than animal
> “She moves faster than an animal.”

For “more and more,” repeat:

> **Sheq-du wa sheq-du…** - “colder and colder…”

## Making adjectives from nouns: -si

Add **-si** to a noun:

> `ikhel` “death” → `ikhel-si` “deadly”

The tale once uses `-si` on an adjective for manner (`budai` “slow” → `budai-si` “slowly”). That extension is still settling.

## Making adjectives with valir

`valir` “of, related to” also builds “related-to-X” adjectives, usually as one word:

> `radyävuvalir` “childish”, `tyevalir` “animalistic”, `qinvalir` “royal”, `qäinvalir` “motherly”

Rough guide: **-si** often means “characterized by X” (deadly = brings death); **valir** often means “pertaining to / like X” (childish = child-like). When unsure, copy an existing word.
