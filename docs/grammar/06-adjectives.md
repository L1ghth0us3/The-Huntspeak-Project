# 6. Adjectives and Comparison

Adjectives stand **before** the noun they describe (`wakh radyävu` "a young child") and become predicates with the copula prefix `sh-` (`sha-sil` "is dark"; chapter 4).

## Comparative and superlative

Two suffixes:

- **-du** - comparative ("more X, X-er")
- **-vu** - superlative ("most X, X-est")

The standard of comparison is introduced by **tiau** "than":

> **Kharau hâiru sha-sil-du tiau nätum.**
> this cave is-dark-er than night
> "This cave is darker than the night."

> **Su amî qar-vu sha-shidyävu.**
> you my good-est be-friend
> "You are my best friend."

Comparatives also work adverbially after the verb:

> **Se nenad shai-du tiau tye.**
> she moves fast-er than animal
> "She moves faster than an animal."

Intensification by repetition is attested in the traditional tale:

> **Sheq-du wa sheq-du…** - "colder and colder…"

## Making adjectives from nouns: -si

Add **-si** to a noun to get an adjective:

> `ikhel` "death" → `ikhel-si` "deadly"

The tale also uses `-si` on an adjective to make an adverb of manner (`budai` "slow" → `budai-si` "slowly"). That extension is attested once and is provisional.

## Making adjectives with valir

`valir` "of, related to" also builds "related-to-X" adjectives, usually written as one word:

> `radyävuvalir` "childish", `tyevalir` "animalistic", `qinvalir` "royal", `qäinvalir` "motherly"

**Division of labor** (as currently attested): `-si` derives a property *characterized by* the noun (deadly = bringing death), while `valir`-forms mean *pertaining to / typical of* the noun (childish = child-like). The boundary is not yet sharply defined; when in doubt follow existing words.
