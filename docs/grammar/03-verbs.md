# 3. Verbs: Roots, Person, and Tense

This chapter covers regular verbs. The small set of irregular "prefix verbs" (be, have, do, hunt…) is chapter 4.

## Consonantal roots

Nearly all Huntspeak verbs are a skeleton of consonants - usually three, sometimes two (official canon):

- `v-d-b` - to write
- `r-s-sh` - to fight
- `l-b-` - to eat (two consonants)

The root alone is not a word. To use a verb you slot two vowels into it:

```
  C₁ V₁ C₂ V₂ C₃
  v  î  d  a  b   →  vîdab
```

- **V₁ (first vowel) tells you WHO** - it copies the vowel of the subject pronoun.
- **V₂ (second vowel) tells you WHEN** - it marks tense and aspect.

With a two-consonant root, the tense vowel simply ends the word: `l-b-` → `lîba` "I eat".

## V₁: the person vowel

The first vowel mimics the subject pronoun's vowel:

| Subject | V₁ | "write" (present) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| î (I) | î | î v**î**dab |
| su (you) | u | su v**u**dab |
| se (he/she/it) | e | se v**e**dab |
| tî (we) | î | tî v**î**dab |
| tu (you pl.) | u | tu v**u**dab |
| te (they) | e | te v**e**dab |

Because the vowel alone can identify a third-person subject, the pronoun is often dropped: **Tye nenad** "the animal moves" - the *se* is implied in n**e**nad.

> **Open question (D11):** the official source's pronoun list writes the three plural forms with *e* in the tense slot (`tî vîdeb` etc.) while glossing them as present, contradicting its own tense rule. This project follows the *a* = present rule for all persons until the project lead rules otherwise.

## V₂: tense and aspect

Huntspeak has four tenses, and each comes in two aspects (project canon, expanding the official three-way a/e/ʌ system):

| | Perfective (single, complete event) | Imperfective (ongoing over a timeframe) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | -a- | -aa- |
| **Past recent** (within about a week) | -e- | -ea- |
| **Past long** (older than that) | -u- | -ua- |
| **Future** | -â- | -âa- |

**Perfective aspect** presents an event as a complete whole ("I fought"). **Imperfective aspect** presents it as ongoing ("I was fighting").

With `r-s-sh` "to fight" and the subject `î`:

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| î rîsash | I fight |
| î rîsesh | I fought (recently) |
| î rîsush | I fought (long ago) |
| î rîsâsh | I will fight |
| î rîsâash | I will be fighting |

From the traditional tale, with past-long imperfective:

> **Tî sîluav wa qîhuap shilaqar.**
> we survive-PAST.LONG.IMPF and help-PAST.LONG.IMPF tribe
> "We survived and helped the tribe (over that whole time)."

## The irrealis particle iqe

**iqe** marks an event as imagined, possible, or conditional - "if / might / would". It is **not a tense**: it stands directly before the verb, and the verb keeps its normal tense and aspect:

> **î dæl iqe rîsesh** - "if I fought a lot"
> **î dæl iqe rîsâsh** - "if I will fight a lot"
> **î iqe vaqash su** - "if I love you"

Conditional sentences and the negative `naaq-iqe` "unless" are covered in chapter 10.

*(Older material called `iqe` the "hypothetical tense" and sometimes spelled it `ike`; both are superseded - decisions D3, D5.)*
