# 3. Verbs: Roots, Person, and Tense

This chapter is regular verbs. Everyday irregulars (“be”, “have”, “do”, “hunt”…) are chapter 4.

## Consonantal roots

Most verbs are a skeleton of consonants - usually three, sometimes two:

- `v-d-b` - to write
- `r-s-sh` - to fight
- `l-b-` - to eat (two consonants)

The root alone isn’t a word. You slot two vowels in:

```
  C₁ V₁ C₂ V₂ C₃
  v  î  d  a  b   →  vîdab
```

- **V₁ (first vowel) = WHO** - copies the subject pronoun’s vowel.
- **V₂ (second vowel) = WHEN** - tense and aspect.

Two-consonant roots put the tense vowel at the end: `l-b-` → `lîba` “I eat”.

## V₁: the person vowel

| Subject | V₁ | “write” (present) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| î (I) | î | î v**î**dab |
| su (you) | u | su v**u**dab |
| se (he/she/it) | e | se v**e**dab |
| tî (we) | î | tî v**î**dab |
| tu (you pl.) | u | tu v**u**dab |
| te (they) | e | te v**e**dab |

Third person often drops the pronoun: **Tye nenad** “the animal moves” - the *e* in n**e**nad already says “it.”

> **Note:** One official chart writes plural presents with *e* in the tense slot while calling them present. We teach **a = present** for all persons until that gets an explicit ruling.

## V₂: tense and aspect

Four tenses, each with two aspects:

| | Perfective (complete event) | Imperfective (ongoing) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | -a- | -aa- |
| **Past recent** (about a week) | -e- | -ea- |
| **Past long** (older) | -u- | -ua- |
| **Future** | -â- | -âa- |

With `r-s-sh` “to fight” and subject `î`:

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| î rîsash | I fight |
| î rîsesh | I fought (recently) |
| î rîsush | I fought (long ago) |
| î rîsâsh | I will fight |
| î rîsâash | I will be fighting |

From the traditional tale (past-long imperfective):

> **Tî sîluav wa qîhuap shilaqar.**
> we survive-PAST.LONG.IMPF and help-PAST.LONG.IMPF tribe
> “We survived and helped the tribe (over that whole time).”

## The irrealis particle iqe

**iqe** marks something imagined, possible, or conditional - “if / might / would”. It is **not a tense**. Put it right before the verb; the verb keeps its normal tense:

> **î dæl iqe rîsesh** - “if I fought a lot”
> **î dæl iqe rîsâsh** - “if I will fight a lot”
> **î iqe vaqash su** - “if I love you”

Conditionals and `naaq-iqe` “unless” are in chapter 10. (Older notes called this a “hypothetical tense” or spelled it `ike` - use **iqe**.)
