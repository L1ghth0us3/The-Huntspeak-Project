# 5. Nouns and Number

## No plural form

Huntspeak nouns do not change for number. `tye` is "animal" or "animals"; context decides.

When the amount matters, say it explicitly, before the noun:

- **with a numeral:** `bi idwa` "two ears"
- **with a quantifier:** `däl` "many", `qei` "few", `yad` "all", `yaqa` "every"

> **Î vîset däl tye yat wäwi.**
> I saw many animal in forest
> "I saw many animals in the forest."

## The pause rule

If number-marking makes two identical vowels touch, they are kept apart by a small pause, written with a hyphen:

> **Su vusat amî bi-idwa.** [bi.idʋa]
> "You see my two ears."

## Numerals

Only the numbers one through five are established:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| u | one |
| bi | two |
| ta | three |
| qu | four |
| ha | five |

Counting above five is an open design question — see `development/gaps.md`. Ordering words exist for the ends of a sequence: `rab` "first", `nääq` "last".

## "One of the..."

The tale attests a partitive pattern with `valir` "of":

> **U valir däl qäin vu-wâmal Zaiya.**
> one of many mother have-PAST.LONG-name Zaiya
> "One of the many mothers had the name Zaiya."
