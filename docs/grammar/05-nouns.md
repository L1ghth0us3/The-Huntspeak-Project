# 5. Nouns and Number

## No plural form

Nouns don’t change for number. `tye` is “animal” or “animals” - context decides.

When amount matters, put it **before** the noun:

- **numeral:** `bi idwa` “two ears”
- **quantifier:** `däl` “many”, `qei` “few”, `yad` “all”, `yaqa` “every”

> **Î vîset däl tye yat wäwi.**
> I saw many animal in forest
> “I saw many animals in the forest.”

## The pause rule

If two identical vowels bump into each other, keep a small pause (written with a hyphen):

> **Su vusat amî bi-idwa.** [bi.idʋa]
> “You see my two ears.”

## Numerals

Numbers one through five are settled:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| u | one |
| bi | two |
| ta | three |
| qu | four |
| ha | five |

Counting past five is still an open design question. For ends of a sequence you’ll also meet `rab` “first” and `nääq` “last”.

## “One of the…”

The tale shows a partitive with `valir` “of”:

> **U valir däl qäin vu-wâmal Zaiya.**
> one of many mother have-PAST.LONG-name Zaiya
> “One of the many mothers had the name Zaiya.”
