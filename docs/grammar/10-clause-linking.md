# 10. Clause Linking

Once you can make a simple sentence, you’ll want to join them: *and*, *but*, *because*, *if*, *while*. Huntspeak keeps each linked clause finite — its own subject and conjugated verb — unless the subject is clearly shared and dropped.

## Coordination: wa, ara, ura

- **wa** — and
- **ara** — but
- **ura** — or

They can join nouns, phrases, or whole clauses. Shared subject? Don’t repeat it:

> **Tî sîluav wa qîhuap shilaqar.** — “We survived and helped the tribe.”

New subject? State it before its verb: *Subject₁ Predicate₁ wa Subject₂ Predicate₂.*

**ara** contrasts two equal statements:

> **Î dîqab ara î rîsash.** — “I am afraid, but I fight.”
> **Î dîqab ara rîsash.** — same, without repeating *î*.

## Event chaining

Closely related events can sit side by side with no `wa`:

> **Î vîsat tye, î rîsash.** — “I see the animal, (then) I fight.”
> **Î vîsat tye, rîsash.** — same, subject omitted.

Read chaining as one episode in order — not “because,” “although,” or “if.”

## Cause: naru — Result: narudi

**naru** = “because”:

> **Î dîqab naru tye sha-turam.** — “I am afraid because the animal is big.”
> **Naru tye sha-turam, î dîqab.** — “Because the animal is big, I am afraid.”

**narudi** = “therefore, so”:

> **Tye sha-turam; narudi î dîqab.** — “The animal is big; therefore I am afraid.”

Same relation, opposite direction: *A naru B* = “A, because B”; *B; narudi A* = “B; therefore A”.

## Concession: qesha

**qesha** = “although, even though”:

> **Qesha î dîqab, î rîsash.** — “Although I am afraid, I fight.”
> **Î rîsash qesha î dîqab.** — “I fight although I am afraid.”

`ara` keeps both clauses equal; `qesha` backgrounds one of them.

## Condition: iqe and naaq-iqe

**iqe** (chapter 3) can turn a clause into an “if”:

> **Iqe shiä sha-led, tye nenad.** — “If the moon is bright, the animal moves.”
> **Tye nenad iqe shiä sha-led.** — result emphasized.

Usually put the condition first. Open vs. counterfactual comes from tense and context.

**naaq-iqe** = “unless”:

> **Naaq-iqe shiä sha-led, tî mîda.** — “Unless the moon is bright, we sleep.”

## Purpose: wey

**wey** (also “for”) introduces an intended outcome — “so that”:

> **Î nîtar qäru wey radyävu leba qäru.** — “I cook food so that the child eats.”

Purpose (`wey`) is not the same as real-world cause (`naru`):

> **Î nîtar qäru naru radyävu sha-qik'et.** — “I cook food because the child is hungry.”

## Time linkage: eshu, avikh, tarem, udil

- **eshu** “while”: **Eshu se meda, î vîdab yasyi.** — “While she sleeps, I write a tale.”
- **avikh** “after”: **Avikh tî rîsash, tî lîba qäru.** — “After we fight, we eat food.”
- **tarem** “before”: **Tarem tî mîda, tî bîsha.** — “Before we sleep, we pray.”
- **udil** “then, next”: **Î vîsat tye, udil rîsash.** — “I see the animal, then fight.”

Frame first or second — both work. `udil` is sequence in time; `narudi` is logical consequence.

## Summary of connectives

| Word | Function | Approximate English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wa | addition | and |
| ara | equal contrast | but |
| ura | alternative | or |
| naru | cause | because |
| narudi | result | therefore, so |
| qesha | concession | although |
| iqe | irrealis / condition | if, might, would |
| naaq-iqe | negative condition | unless |
| wey | purpose | so that, in order to |
| eshu | simultaneous time | while |
| avikh | later time | after |
| tarem | earlier time | before |
| udil | explicit sequence | then, next |
