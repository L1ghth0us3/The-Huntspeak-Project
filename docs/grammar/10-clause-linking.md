# 10. Clause Linking

Huntspeak distinguishes **coordination** (joining equals), **event chaining** (sequences of connected events), and **subordination** (one clause as cause, condition, purpose, time frame, or background of another).

Unless stated otherwise, every linked clause stays a complete finite clause: it keeps its own subject and conjugated predicate.

## Coordination: wa, ara, ura

- **wa** - and
- **ara** - but
- **ura** - or

A coordinator may join nouns, phrases, predicates, or whole clauses. When coordinated predicates share a subject, the subject is not repeated (each predicate stays fully conjugated):

> **Tî sîluav wa qîhuap shilaqar.** - "We survived and helped the tribe."

When the subject changes, state the new subject before its predicate: *Subject₁ Predicate₁ wa Subject₂ Predicate₂.*

**ara** joins two contrasting statements of equal weight; neither is subordinate:

> **Î dîqab ara î rîsash.** - "I am afraid, but I fight."
> **Î dîqab ara rîsash.** - same, with the repeated subject omitted.

## Event chaining

Closely connected events may simply stand side by side, without `wa`:

> **Î vîsat tye, î rîsash.** - "I see the animal, (then) I fight."
> **Î vîsat tye, rîsash.** - same, subject omitted.

Chained events are understood to belong to one episode, happen in the order stated, and keep the same subject unless a new one is introduced. Chaining implies sequence or close connection only - never "because", "although", or "if".

## Cause: naru - Result: narudi

**naru** introduces a reason ("because"):

> **Î dîqab naru tye sha-turam.** - "I am afraid because the animal is big."
> **Naru tye sha-turam, î dîqab.** - "Because the animal is big, I am afraid." (cause first = sets the context)

**narudi** introduces a consequence ("therefore, so"):

> **Tye sha-turam; narudi î dîqab.** - "The animal is big; therefore I am afraid."

They express the same relation from opposite directions: *A naru B* = "A, because B"; *B; narudi A* = "B; therefore A". (`narudi` grew historically out of `naru` + `udil`, but is a separate word.)

## Concession: qesha

**qesha** = "although, even though" - a circumstance that might be expected to prevent the main event:

> **Qesha î dîqab, î rîsash.** - "Although I am afraid, I fight."
> **Î rîsash qesha î dîqab.** - "I fight although I am afraid."

Difference from `ara`: `ara` presents two equal assertions; `qesha` demotes one to background, so the main assertion is the other clause.

## Condition: iqe and naaq-iqe

The irrealis particle **iqe** (chapter 3) turns a clause into a condition when linked to a result clause:

> **Iqe shiä sha-led, tye nenad.** - "If the moon is bright, the animal moves."
> **Tye nenad iqe shiä sha-led.** - result emphasized.

The condition normally comes first. Whether a condition is open or counterfactual is carried by tense and context.

**naaq-iqe** = "unless, if not" (a fixed compound, written with the hyphen):

> **Naaq-iqe shiä sha-led, tî mîda.** - "Unless the moon is bright, we sleep."

## Purpose: wey

**wey** (also the preposition "for") introduces an intended outcome - "so that, in order to":

> **Î nîtar qäru wey radyävu leba qäru.** - "I cook food so that the child eats."

Purpose (intended, maybe unachieved, `wey`) is distinct from cause (actual reason, `naru`):

> **Î nîtar qäru naru radyävu sha-qik'et.** - "I cook food because the child is hungry."

## Time linkage: eshu, avikh, tarem, udil

- **eshu** "while": **Eshu se meda, î vîdab yasyi.** - "While she sleeps, I write a tale."
- **avikh** "after": **Avikh tî rîsash, tî lîba qäru.** - "After we fight, we eat food."
- **tarem** "before": **Tarem tî mîda, tî bîsha.**, "Before we sleep, we pray." (`tarem` is purely temporal, distinct from spatial `dakhan` "in front of")
- **udil** "then, next" makes a sequence explicit: **Î vîsat tye, udil rîsash.** - "I see the animal, then fight."

Each temporal clause may come first (setting the frame) or second (adding the frame afterwards).

`udil` (chronological sequence) is not `narudi` (logical consequence): two events can follow each other without one causing the other, that is `udil`.

## Summary of connectives

| Word | Function | Approximate English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wa | addition | and |
| ara | equal contrast | but |
| ura | alternative | or |
| naru | cause | because |
| narudi | result | therefore, so |
| qesha | concession | although |
| iqe | irrealis / condition | if, might, would |
| naaq-iqe | negative condition | unless |
| wey | purpose | so that, in order to |
| eshu | simultaneous time | while |
| avikh | later time | after |
| tarem | earlier time | before |
| udil | explicit sequence | then, next |
